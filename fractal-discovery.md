## Framing refinement (BUILT ckpt 76, both homes, ON by default; mechanics → `curation/README.md` / `curation/framing.py`)
- The location head picks FRAMING within one location on the seating statistic; **adopt only on a strict LOG-ODDS margin (Δ = 2.0 nats, Matt by eye) — a probability margin adopts nothing where P(≥4) pins near 1.** Gate-refused framings are never adoptable. `framing.MARGIN` STAYS AT 2.0 (Matt chose simpler over raising it; 67.8% of 28k locations adopt). A scan row at another margin is REFUSED rather than reinterpreted, and anything hunted at 2.0 stays valid if the margin later moves.

Framing happens FIRST, eventually BY THE RUNS, with locations born already-framed (Matt, ckpt 84) — which DISSOLVES the migration rather than scheduling it.** There is no migration to plan, no re-embed to schedule and no viewport question to rule; the shipped geometry becomes the refined one and the two identity conventions converge on their own.

Every refusal is `below_margin`, so the adoption rate reads on FRAMINGS, not coverage. Price is its own report bucket `refine_minutes`, never folded into `reframe_minutes`.

## Novelty & run design (RULED ckpt 62–63)
- **★★★ THE NOVELTY DIRECTIVE (Matt): "a 1% success rate in a novel region is better than the 1000th beautiful image in Seahorse valley."** Four levers, all BUILT (→ `supply/README.md`): per-lineage supply caps · saturation discount · a protected exploration share · seat-to-budget. All are PACING, never rejection.
- **★★ THE SHARE IS A SWITCH, NOT A PRICE:** never-walked lineages book far fewer admissions than the contest (the heads' novelty blindness), so the FLOOR is the whole policy (0.25 ratified) and **the binding constraint is novel-lineage STOCK, not the fraction.** The share buys ground the heads score as junk, by design. Julia is CLOSED to share-novelty — its lever is c-spacing REFILL. Lineage-level identity does not capture gallery-level novelty.
- **★★ Saturation identity = LINEAGE/root-neighborhood FIRST** (palette never counts — geometry is the identity); descriptor clusters are the reserved fallback (parked).
- **★★ THE LAST GATE IS THE SOLVE (Matt, ckpt 83; BUILT ckpt 84):** a run only feeds the pool; diversity, colour and mode are decided ONCE, as constraints on the SET rather than filters on a seating order → `preserve\selection_design.md`. Cite, never restate. **★★ BREADTH BUYS CONSTRAINT SATISFACTION, DEPTH BUYS QUALITY** — a 644-candidate hunt into the unopened pool closed a colour shortage and did not move the n=20 gallery by a digit. **Mining, not optimizing, is what raises the floor.** The diversity radius is PARTITION-BLIND today and the only near-duplicate pairs found were cross-partition, which closed the parked cross-partition-radius item. **★★ THE TWIN GRAPH DENSIFIES AS THE POOL GROWS (ckpt 88)** — over one doubling of places, 89% → 92% of places sit in at least one twin pair and mean degree among the touched went 9.94 → 12.83. The necessary upper bound on non-twin capacity moves freely while the CONSTRUCTIVE lower bound (a greedy independent set) barely does — 447 → 532 against an upper of 1,038 — so most of the widening is slack in the bound, not proven capacity. Each new place is likelier to be somebody's twin than the last, and **a thousand-seat gallery is still unproven from below.**
- Budget semantics: a run's budget is a TARGET, not a cap — re-seat rather than stop early (→ fractal-tutorial for `--finish-by`). DEPTH/DEEP vocabulary → settled_rulings.

## Mining economics (MEASURED ckpt 85)
- **★★★ EVERY "PRIMED AT k" COUNT IS A MAXIMUM OVER k NOISY SCORES AND IS OPTIMISTIC.** The multiplier at the 0.90 bar is **k-dependent: ×1.00 at k=10, ×0.71 at k=20, ×0.70 at k=40** — so it applies at k≥20 and barely touches counts mined at k=3 or k=12. Quote a prime count as RAW or CALIBRATED and never mix them; the gap grows with how hard we mine.
- **★★ PALETTES ARE EXCHANGEABLE WITHIN THE UNRESTRICTED POOL, TO k=40** — the per-candidate clear rate is flat in k with no ordering effect, so the palette head's rank buys nothing at the boundary and k is bounded by cost, never by taste. **⚠ A CARRIER-RESTRICTED SUBSET IS NOT EXCHANGEABLE WITH IT (ckpt 88).**
- **★★ CONDITIONING MOVES THE COLOUR COLUMN AND COSTS THE QUALITY ONE (MEASURED ckpt 88).** On `light_vivid_teal`, 127 hitting locations and 36 hitting maps an arm: cell hit rate **0.4589 against a matched flat control's 0.0197 (23.3×)** at 2.18 renders a hit, and **clear rate 1.41% [1.0–1.8] against 4.70% [4.0–5.5]** — non-overlapping. The composed price still favours conditioning **9.0× on renders and 7.1× on seconds**. The earlier 17.0× / 1.26 renders / 32 maps is `lav2` on `dark_vivid_green` at five locations an arm — not a teal result and not a wide one.
- **★★★ IT IS THE MAP SET, NOT THE COLOUR.** Split each arm by what the picture actually came out: inside the conditioned arm, teal rows clear at 1.29% and non-teal rows at 1.52%; inside the flat arm, 3.33% and 4.73%. **Coming out teal costs nothing; being drawn from the carrier set costs everything** — the carrier set's own *non-teal* rows clear at 1.52% against the general pool's 4.73%. The maps that carry a cell are worse maps whatever colour they land on, and the damage falls on the three modes with a real `P(≥4)` bar (4–11×) while the `P(≥3)`-fallback modes are flat or better. The carriers are not making unusable pictures — they are making pictures the FOURTH cutpoint declines.
- **A themed collection is affordable at reachable scale**: roughly 2.5 hours of render per 60 places, bought by paying for a much worse score distribution and taking its thin top (arm p90 `P(≥4)` 0.0266 against a control's 0.1084). Draw biased, render, read the dominance the row already carries, keep what came out dominant — the carrier table is a prior, not a gate.
- **⚠ THE LEDGER-WIDE CLEAR RATE IS NOT A DRAW RATE.** 6.96% over the whole ledger is dominated by near-band and mode-floor draws on already-proven places; a fresh breadth draw on never-opened locations clears **4.70%**, and that is what a new arm is read against. Untargeted, a green picture costs 1.8–6× a blue one and a green picture the judge likes costs 13–18×.
- **★★ DEPTH SUBSTITUTES FOR RANK BUT DOES NOT REPLACE IT.** The location head's rank spreads the prime rate 4.0× end to end (~2.4× pooled) — draw the top half, not the whole range. **CHOOSING THE MODE ROSTER IS WORTH MORE THAN RANK (~2×)**, and the per-mode field-dump cost spreads across the roster (`smooth` cheapest), which is the arithmetic that decision is made on. **⚠ THE SPREAD IS A PROPERTY OF THE ROSTER DRAWN, NOT A CONSTANT (ckpt 87): 5.8× on a field-only breadth roster and 13.5× on a full roster.** The older 7× figure sits between two populations and matches neither — read it off the run whose roster you are pricing, never from a doc. `tia` is near-band only, never drawn at k=20. **⚠ `trap_circle` WILL NOT BE MINED TO 25 AND DOES NOT NEED TO BE (ckpt 88):** at a 0.17% clear rate it is an order of magnitude below every other mode — 1,439 places bought 9 — and reaching 25 needs ~2,200 more places, about 9 hours. Same fact that demoted it at ckpt 84: the supply is not there and the draw was not starved. It still moved `mode_floors` from short at n=300 to short at n=1,200 by going 2 → 11 places, because the binding mode is the thinnest one. **The 25-place flag and the mode floor are different questions.**
- **★★ "CLEARED" MEANS A `P(≥4) ≥ 0.50` CROSSING AND SAYS NOTHING ABOUT HUMAN q3s.** `gaussian_int` and `smooth_trap_circle` gained no seat at proven locations and only 11 of 49 mode-floor seats filled, but no instrument looks below that bar, so a mode's q3 supply is UNMEASURED. Both stay admitted — novelty is worth a 3 (Matt, ckpt 86). The judge-side reason → fractal-corpus §Judge method.
- **⚠ THE FLAT-VS-RANKED COST GAP IS REAL, LOCALIZED, AND TWO DIFFERENT NUMBERS (ckpt 87).** On the last run with a genuine flat control it is **2.24× on the mean and 1.49× on the median** of the same population — quoting one hides which. Mode mix explains none of it (standardising either way leaves 2.3×; 16 of 18 modes show it within mode) and maxiter explains none (flat's mean is *lower*). The stage split settles it: **every engine stage is 2–3× dearer and every Python stage is flat**, so it is realized iteration per pixel — the location head's rank is correlated with locations that are cheap to iterate. On a much larger population read band-against-band it is only **1.11×**. Both are true and they answer different questions.
